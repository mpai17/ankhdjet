One transistor per weight, mask-programmed to +1, −1, or 0. Fire one wordline at a time, read every column's bitline at once, and fold the multi-bit activations in bit-plane by bit-plane. The multiply is a wire; the accumulate is shift-and-add.

The multiply is free

A cell's via decides which bitline it pulls: +1 to BL+, −1 to BL−, 0 to neither. Firing the wordline reads all columns in parallel — that is the whole "multiply by ±1."

Bit-serial activations

Activations are multi-bit, so each row is folded in one bit-plane at a time. Plane k contributes its hits shifted left by k (weight 2k), gated by that activation bit.

Shift-and-add is the tree

No real multiplier: each cycle adds or subtracts a shifted column of hits into the per-column accumulator. After every row and plane, acc[c] holds the exact signed dot product.
